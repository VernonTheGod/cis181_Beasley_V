South Side · 1950

Harold's Chicken

Harold Pierce came to Chicago from Alabama in 1943. On June 22, 1950, he opened Harold's Chicken Shack on the South Side. He and his wife, Hilda, had already run a small restaurant called H&H on 39th Street, where they served dumplings and chicken feet.

What people still argue about

Writers do not agree on the exact first corner. Some say 47th and Kenwood. Others say Greenwood. This page will not pretend one address is settled. What is settled is the plate: fried chicken, usually over fries and a slice or two of white bread, with mild sauce or hot sauce.

A lot of regulars say "fried hard." That means they want the crust darker and crunchier than a normal fry. Locations were also allowed to feel different from each other. Some still serve through a window. Pierce did not force every shack to look the same.

A first order

If you have never been, start with a mixed half chicken, mild sauce, fries, and white bread. Mixed means both white and dark meat. Mild sauce is the sweet, tangy Chicago sauce. Hot sauce is sharper. You can also ask for sauce on the side.

The business is still in the family. Harold Pierce's daughter, Kristen Pierce, has led the company.
