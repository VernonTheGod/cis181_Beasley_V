West Side · 1969

Uncle Remus

Gus Rickette and Mary Rickette came north from Mississippi. They ran earlier chicken shacks on the West Side, including G&G Chicken. The first restaurant under the Uncle Remus name opened in February 1969 at 210 S. Cicero Avenue.

The name

The name did not start as a tribute to the old folk-tale character. Gus Rickette has said it came from a restaurant sign a sign maker sold at a discount after another owner walked away from it. The Madison Street shop in Austin, at 5611 W. Madison, opened in August 1969 and is still part of the story.

Mild sauce and a first order

The signature plate is wings with fries and mild sauce. The sauce became the house style after customers kept mixing ketchup and hot sauce on their own. The chicken is double-battered. The business stayed in the family. Their daughter, Charmaine Rickette, later ran the company.

Uncle Remus is the West Side answer in the Chicago chicken argument. Harold's is the older South Side shack. The mild sauce is what the two have in common.
